# Ethical hacking: foundations and authorization

Source: Unit 1 slides 4–28. Lecture reference, not verified lab execution.

Information security manages confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity and non-repudiation. A threat can exploit a vulnerability through an attack vector; risk reflects likelihood and impact. Protect assets through access controls, verified software, backups and awareness. Offensive testing without explicit permission is prohibited regardless of intent.

The five described phases—reconnaissance, scanning, gaining access, maintaining access and clearing tracks—explain attacker behavior. In authorized testing, exploitation and persistence require specific approval; deleting evidence or hiding unauthorized activity is not an ethical deliverable. Black-box work starts with little system knowledge, white-box with extensive knowledge, and gray-box with partial knowledge.

An engagement plan identifies the owner, in-scope assets, exclusions, allowed methods and timing, rate limits, contacts, evidence handling, emergency-stop criteria and reporting process. A vulnerability assessment identifies and prioritizes weaknesses; penetration testing may also validate impact. A scan result alone is not compromise evidence.

The slides include adversary motivations, phishing, malware, cloud/IoT, application and AI threats and the professional need for networking, OS, scripting, reporting and communications skills. See the deeper scanning and assessment files alongside the existing topic README.
